What is a "Babbitt Spike" and Lead Time Verification for Event 2:
  • What is Babbitt?: "Babbitt metal" is a tin-antimony-copper soft alloy lining the contact shell of hydrodynamic sleeve bearings. It supports the rotating shaft on a pressurized oil film. Babbitt softens above 120°C and melts at ~230°C. API 670 specifies bearing trips at 115°C - 120°C, and the DCS trip limit in operating_limits.db is configured at BT_HH = 140.3°C.
  • What is a Babbitt Spike?: An acute, steep temperature rise in the bearing metal thermocouple caused by lube oil film collapse, oil starvation, metal-to-metal rubbing, or severe friction shear (or a sensor grounding/junction fault).
  • Exact Timestamp of Trip Breach: At 06:00:00 UTC, the Digital Twin triggered an early warning (score 42,082 > 732) predicting Impending Bearing Babbitt Metal High-Temperature Trip (BT_HH). At exactly 07:42:00 UTC, Bearing 4 rocketed to 795.58°C (exceeding the 140.3°C DCS limit), lasting 8 minutes until 07:49 UTC before resetting.
  • Actual Lead Time: Exactly 1 hour 42 minutes (102 minutes) of advance notice before the hard DCS trip breach occurred. (The advisory table's 48.0h represents the catalog's standard MTBF degradation horizon).
